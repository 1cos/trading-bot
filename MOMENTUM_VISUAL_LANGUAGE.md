# Momentum Visual Language

> **Purpose:** Describe how Max visually recognizes momentum before
> defining any measurement. Written for a discretionary trader, not
> a programmer.
> **Source:** OCL_MOMENTUM_DISCOVERY.md and the 15 synthetic examples.
> **Not included:** Definitions, thresholds, formulas, or implementation.

---

## What Max Is Observing

### The move catches the eye immediately

Momentum is not something you need to search for. When you glance at
the chart, the move is already obvious. The candles are marching in
one direction and the chart looks tilted. You do not need to zoom in
or count anything. If you have to squint to see it, it is not momentum.

### The candles are covering ground

Each candle takes the price somewhere new. The close of one candle is
meaningfully farther from where the sequence started than the candle
before it. The market is not chewing through the same range over and
over. It is leaving territory behind and not coming back for it.

### The bodies tell the story, not the wicks

In a momentum move, the candle bodies are large and the wicks are
small. The price opens, picks a direction, and commits to it. It does
not explore both sides of the candle before deciding. The body fills
most of the candle's range. When you look at the sequence, you see
solid blocks of color stacking on top of each other, not thin lines
with long shadows.

### Each candle picks up where the last one stopped

There is no gap backward. Each candle opens near the close of the
previous one and pushes further. The move stacks. The bodies do not
overlap significantly — each one occupies new price space. The
sequence looks like steps on a staircase, not a bouncing ball.

### The move feels effortless

Momentum does not look like a fight. There is no back-and-forth
negotiation between buyers and sellers. One side is in control and
the other side has stepped away. The candles flow in one direction
without resistance. The move looks like the market is falling in the
direction of gravity rather than being pushed.

### The first candle already has intent

The move does not start with a small hesitant candle that gradually
builds into something. The very first candle of the sequence is already
large and committed. The market declares its direction immediately.
There is no warmup period.

### The candles are staying the same size or getting bigger

As the move progresses, the candles are either maintaining their size
or expanding. The market is accelerating or sustaining — never fading.
When you see the second candle is as large or larger than the first,
the move is alive. The market is not running out of energy.

---

## What Max Is NOT Observing

### There is no alternation

Momentum does not alternate between up candles and down candles. If
you see red-green-red-green, the market is negotiating, not moving.
A momentum sequence is the same color, unbroken, until the One Candle
interrupts it.

### There is no compression

Momentum is the opposite of a tight range. If the candles are getting
smaller and pressing into each other, the market is coiling, not
moving. Compression looks like the chart is being squeezed. Momentum
looks like it is being released.

### The candles are not shrinking

When the bodies start getting smaller with each successive candle, the
move is losing conviction. A sequence where the first candle is large,
the second is medium, and the third is small is a move that is running
out of breath. It may still be pointing in the right direction, but the
energy is draining. This is drift, not impulse.

### There is no overlap

When consecutive candle bodies overlap — when the body of one candle
sits inside the range of the previous one — the market is going
sideways even if the candles happen to be the same color. Real momentum
does not revisit what it just covered. It leaves it behind.

### The chart does not look flat

If you zoom out and the price is in the same area where it was several
candles ago, there is no momentum. Momentum produces visible
displacement on the chart. The price is somewhere clearly different
from where it started. The move has a slope you can see without drawing
a line.

### There is no hesitation at the start

Momentum does not begin with a few uncertain candles that eventually
find their footing. If the first candle is small and tentative, the
sequence is not an impulse — it is a slow build. A slow build may
eventually become momentum, but at the moment the One Candle appears,
the move before it should already look decisive.

## The Simplest Test

If another trader walked past your screen and glanced at the chart for
two seconds, would they say "something is happening"? If yes, there is
momentum. If they would walk past without noticing, there is not.

This is not a definition. It is the starting point for discovery. When
real market examples are available, we will look at the same
characteristics and see which ones survive contact with actual price
action.
