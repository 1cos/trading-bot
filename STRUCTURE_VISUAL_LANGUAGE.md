# Structure Visual Language

> **Purpose:** Describe how Max visually recognizes that price has
> reached an important area, before any thought of entry.
> Written for a discretionary trader, not a programmer.
> **Not included:** Indicators, rules, thresholds, or implementation.

---

## What Max Is Observing

### The price has been here before and something happened

The most immediate sign that an area matters is history. Price arrived
at this level in the past and reacted — it reversed, it stalled, it
accelerated away. The area has a memory. When price returns, that
memory creates expectation. A level where nothing ever happened is
just a number. A level where price turned around twice is a place
that means something.

### The reactions are visible without drawing anything

You do not need to add lines to the chart to see where the important
areas are. The turning points are obvious. The candles that reversed
there left marks — long wicks rejecting the level, sharp reversals,
sudden changes of direction. If you have to draw a line first and
then convince yourself the reactions are there, the area is probably
not important enough.

### Yesterday left marks on today's chart

Previous Day High and Previous Day Low are areas the market already
judged as extremes. They represent the boundaries of yesterday's
agreement about value. When today's price reaches those boundaries,
it is re-entering a conversation that the market already had. Traders
remember where yesterday ended. These levels carry weight because they
carry attention.

### The morning left marks on the session

The first minutes of the session create a range. The Opening Range
high and low represent the initial auction — where buyers and sellers
first negotiated after the open. When price later returns to these
boundaries, it is revisiting the opening argument. The significance
comes from timing: the open is when the most participants are paying
attention.

### The pre-market told you where the interest was

Before the regular session, the market already explored a range.
Pre-Market High and Pre-Market Low are the boundaries of that
exploration. They reveal where overnight interest ran out of
conviction. When regular-session price reaches these levels, it is
testing whether the full market agrees with what the overnight
session found.

### Price accelerates away from important areas

One of the clearest signs that an area mattered is what happened after
price left it. If price touched a level and then moved away quickly —
the candles got bigger, the move became effortless, the market stopped
negotiating — that departure confirms the area's importance. The speed
of the exit is proportional to the significance of the level. A slow
drift away means nothing. A fast escape means the level is real.

### Multiple levels stack near the same price

When several different structural references point to the same area —
yesterday's high is near today's opening range high, or a previous
turning point sits near a pre-market boundary — the area becomes
heavier. The convergence is visible: the chart has multiple reasons
to care about the same price. One level is a suggestion. Several
levels at the same place is a statement.

### The area has width, not just a price

Important areas are rarely a single exact price. They are zones. Price
does not react at 105.00 exactly — it reacts somewhere between 104.80
and 105.20. The wicks and bodies of previous reactions define the
thickness of the zone. A trader looking at the chart sees a band, not
a line. The thicker the band of previous reactions, the more
significant the area.

### The rejection was decisive

When price reached the area in the past and was rejected, the
rejection itself tells you how important the area is. A long wick
that snapped back immediately means the level fought hard. A slow,
grinding turn that took several candles means the level held, but
barely. The quality of past rejections shapes the expectation for
the next one.

---

## What Max Is NOT Observing

### There is no reaction in the history

If price passed through a level smoothly in the past — no pause, no
wick, no change of behavior — the level is not structural. A line
on the chart where price simply transited without noticing is
decoration, not structure. Important areas cause visible disturbance
in the price action around them.

### The level was only touched once

A single touch does not establish structure. One reaction could be
coincidence. When the same area causes a reaction on a second or
third visit, the pattern becomes structural. A level that has been
tested once is a hypothesis. A level that has been tested multiple
times is a fact.

### Price passed through without slowing down

If price reached the area and continued through it as if it were not
there, the area has been broken. A broken level may become relevant
again later — old resistance can become new support — but at the
moment of the break, the market has told you it no longer respects
that boundary. The absence of a reaction is information.

### The area only exists because of a line you drew

If the structural significance depends entirely on a trendline, a
Fibonacci level, a moving average, or any other constructed overlay,
it is not structure in Max's sense. Max is looking at what the price
itself did — where it turned, where it rejected, where it stalled.
The structure comes from the candles, not from geometry imposed on
top of them.

### The area is far from the current price

A level that exists thirty points away from where price is now may
be structurally valid, but it is not relevant to the current decision.
Max is observing areas that price is approaching or has just reached.
Distant structure is background context, not an active observation.
The question is about what is happening here, now.

### The candles around the area look ordinary

If you look at the price action near the area and nothing stands out
— no wicks, no reversals, no acceleration, no change of character —
then the area is not important. Important areas produce unusual
candles. The price behaves differently there than it does in the
middle of a range. If the behavior is the same everywhere, there
is no structure.

---

## The Simplest Test

If you removed all lines, all indicators, and all overlays from the
chart, and you looked at the naked candles alone — would you still
point to the same area and say "something happens here"? If yes, it
is structure. If you need the overlay to see it, it is not.

This is not a definition. It is the visual vocabulary for recognizing
where the market has drawn its own boundaries. When real examples are
labeled, we will use this language to describe what Max sees at each
level before any entry decision is made.
