# Max's Trading Decision Flow

> **Purpose:** Describe how Max thinks from the moment the market opens
> until a trade is executed. This is a human mental workflow, not a
> software specification.
> **Not included:** Architecture, indicators, formulas, or code.

---

## Before the Open

### What is Max trying to understand?

Where did yesterday leave off, and what happened overnight.

Max looks at where yesterday's session ended. He notes the high and
the low — not because they are indicators, but because they are the
last places the market expressed an opinion about value. He looks at
pre-market activity to see where overnight interest concentrated. If
the pre-market pushed well above yesterday's high, that means
something different than if it stayed inside yesterday's range.

Max is building a mental map of where the boundaries are before the
first candle prints. He is not predicting direction. He is identifying
the places where the market has already shown it cares.

---

## The Open — First Minutes

### What is Max trying to understand?

How aggressive is the open, and where does the initial auction settle.

The first minutes tell Max what kind of day this might be. A wide,
volatile opening range means the market is unsettled and searching. A
narrow, compressed open means the market is waiting for something. A
one-sided open that immediately pushes in one direction means someone
has already decided.

Max watches the opening range form. He is not trading yet. He is
letting the market show him where the first boundaries are. The high
and low of these first minutes become reference points — not because
of a rule, but because every other participant is also watching them.

---

## After the Open — Reading the Context

### What is Max trying to understand?

Is the market moving with purpose, or is it wandering.

Once the opening range is established, Max watches what happens next.
Does price leave the range decisively, or does it oscillate inside it?
Is there a direction emerging, or is the market chopping? He is
reading the character of the session.

He is also watching how today's price interacts with yesterday's
levels. If price approaches the previous day high, does it hesitate?
Does it punch through? Does it reverse? Each interaction tells him
whether the market respects the boundary or has moved past it.

Max is not looking for a trade yet. He is building context. He needs
to understand what kind of environment he is operating in before any
setup matters.

---

## Momentum Appears

### What is Max trying to understand?

Has the market committed to a direction.

At some point — maybe minutes after the open, maybe much later — Max
sees a move that catches his eye. The candles are covering ground. The
bodies are large. The direction is clear. The market has stopped
negotiating and started moving.

Max is asking himself one question: is this real commitment, or will
it fade? He watches the candles for the signs he knows — are they
stacking, are they maintaining size, is the move effortless? He is
not measuring. He is watching. The difference between a real impulse
and a fake one is something he recognizes visually before he can
articulate why.

---

## The One Candle Appears

### What is Max trying to understand?

Did the momentum just pause for exactly one breath.

Inside the directional move, a single candle prints in the opposite
color. Max notices it immediately because it interrupts the flow. He
is now asking: is this a pause or a reversal? The answer comes from
what happens next.

He looks at the candle itself. Does it have a wick pointing in the
trend direction? That wick is the market briefly pushing further with
the trend before pulling back. It marks a specific price area where
the momentum was still alive inside an opposing candle.

He does not act yet. He notes the candle. He notes the wick. He waits.

---

## Continuation Confirms

### What is Max trying to understand?

Did the trend actually resume, or was the One Candle the beginning
of the end.

After the opposing candle, Max watches the next candles. If the
original direction resumes — same color, same energy, covering ground
again — the One Candle was a pause, not a reversal. The level is born.

If instead the market hesitates, prints more opposing candles, or
stalls, the structure is broken. There is no One Candle Level because
there was no continuation. Max moves on. No frustration, no forcing.
The setup either exists or it does not.

---

## Price Moves Away

### What is Max trying to understand?

Has the market moved far enough from the level that a return would
be meaningful.

The trend continues past the One Candle. Max watches the price move
away from the wick zone. He is waiting for enough distance that if
the price comes back, it would represent a genuine retest — not just
the next candle oscillating around the same area.

He is not counting bars or measuring distance. He is watching whether
the price has clearly left the zone behind. When the One Candle's
wick is no longer near the current price, it has become a level in
the past that price might revisit.

---

## The Wait

### What is Max trying to understand?

Nothing yet. He is being patient.

This is the longest phase. The level exists. The market is somewhere
else. Max is watching other things, other instruments, other setups.
The One Candle Level sits in his awareness, waiting. He does not chase.
He does not anticipate. He lets the market come back to the level on
its own terms, or not at all.

Most levels are never retested. Max accepts this. A good level that
is never revisited is not a failure — it is a setup that did not
trigger. There is nothing to do.

---

## Price Returns to the Level

### What is Max trying to understand?

Is the market coming back to the wick zone.

At some point, price starts moving back toward the One Candle Level.
Max's attention sharpens. He is watching the approach. Is the return
gradual or abrupt? Is it a slow drift back or a sharp pullback? The
character of the approach matters to him, though he has not defined
exactly how yet.

He is not ready to trade. He is getting ready to watch one specific
candle.

---

## The Entry Candle

### What is Max trying to understand?

Does this candle touch the level and reject.

This is the moment everything narrows to a single bar. Max is watching
the current candle form. He is asking: does it reach into the wick
zone? And if it does, does it reject — does it close back in the
direction of the original trend, away from the zone, with conviction?

He is watching for the candle to tell him that the level held. The
price entered the zone and was pushed back out. Not gently, not
ambiguously — decisively. The close of the candle is on the right
side of the level and the body shows that the level fought back.

If the candle touches the zone but closes weakly — small body, no
commitment, indecisive — Max does not trade. The level may still be
valid, but this particular candle did not prove it.

If the candle passes through the zone and closes on the wrong side,
the level has failed. The market does not respect it. Max moves on.

---

## The Decision

### What is Max trying to understand?

Do I believe this.

Everything has aligned: momentum created a One Candle, continuation
confirmed it, price moved away and came back, and the entry candle
touched the zone and rejected. The structure says trade.

Max pauses. He checks his conviction. Does the whole picture make
sense? Is the momentum still the dominant story on the chart, or has
the context changed? Is he trading because the setup is there, or
because he wants to trade?

If everything is coherent, he enters at the close of the entry candle.
If something feels wrong — even if he cannot name it — he waits. There
will be another setup. There is always another setup.

---

## After Entry

### What is Max trying to understand?

Only two things: has the stop been hit, or has the target been reached.

The trade is on. Max is no longer analyzing. He is managing. The stop
is placed. The target is set. He does not move the stop to give the
trade more room. He does not take profits early because he is nervous.
The plan was made before entry. Now he follows it.

The outcome does not change his assessment of the level. A good setup
that gets stopped out was still a good setup. A bad setup that happens
to work was still a bad setup. Max evaluates the process, not the
result.
