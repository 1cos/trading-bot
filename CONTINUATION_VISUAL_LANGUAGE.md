# Continuation Visual Language

> **Purpose:** Describe how Max visually recognizes that the move
> continued after the One Candle. Not how to measure it — what
> it looks like.
> **Not included:** Candle counts, distance thresholds, formulas,
> or implementation.

---

## What Continuation Looks Like

### The trend picks up where it left off

The One Candle was a breath, not a stop. After it, the next candle
opens and immediately pushes in the original direction, as if the
opposing candle never happened. The flow resumes. The interruption
was momentary and the market returns to what it was doing before
without needing to be convinced.

### The candles after look like the candles before

The best continuation feels like the same move restarted. The candle
sizes are similar — large, committed, directional. The energy before
the One Candle and the energy after are the same kind of energy. If
you covered the One Candle with your thumb, the move would look
uninterrupted. The pause was invisible to the trend's character.

### The market accepts higher prices (or lower prices)

In a LONG continuation, each candle after the One Candle prints a
higher low and a higher close. The market is not fighting to stay
up — it is naturally moving up. The higher prices feel accepted, not
forced. In a SHORT continuation, the mirror: lower highs, lower
closes, the market sinking without effort.

### There is no struggle at the One Candle's price

After the One Candle, the price does not hover around the One Candle's
close, debating whether to continue. It leaves that area promptly.
The One Candle's price becomes territory in the rearview mirror, not
a contested zone. If the market is still arguing at the same price
where the One Candle closed, the continuation has not started.

### Price moves away and does not look back

Real continuation puts distance between the current price and the
One Candle. After a few bars, the One Candle is clearly behind. The
price is in new territory. Looking at the chart, the One Candle sits
in the middle of the move, not at the edge. It is surrounded by
trend, not perched at the frontier of uncertainty.

### The move feels like a resumption, not a new beginning

Continuation is not a new impulse starting from scratch. It is the
same impulse continuing. The difference is visible: a new beginning
has its own buildup, its own first uncertain candle, its own
acceleration. Continuation does not need a buildup — it is already
in motion. The first candle after the One Candle is already big,
already committed, already covering ground.

---

## What Continuation Does NOT Look Like

### Immediate stall

The One Candle prints, and then the next candle is small, goes
nowhere, and sits in the same range. The market took a breath and
then forgot to start moving again. The pause was not one candle — it
extended into two, three, or more. What was supposed to be a momentary
interruption became a zone of indecision.

### Immediate reversal

The One Candle prints, and the next candle is another opposing candle.
Then another. The trend did not resume — it broke. The One Candle was
not a pause. It was the first candle of a reversal. Two or more
opposing candles after the One Candle means the structure is gone.

### Loss of conviction

The candles after the One Candle are in the right direction but
visibly weaker. Smaller bodies, longer wicks, less ground covered.
The market is still moving the right way but without the force it had
before. It is dragging itself rather than flowing. The energy has
changed. Before the One Candle the move was effortless; after it, the
move is labored.

### Compression

Instead of resuming the trend, the candles tighten into a narrow
range. The bodies get smaller. The highs and lows converge. The chart
is being squeezed. This is the market coiling, not continuing. It
may eventually break out in the original direction, but at this
moment the continuation has not happened. Compression after the One
Candle means the trend has paused indefinitely, not for one candle.

### Overlapping candles

The candles after the One Candle are trading through each other's
ranges. One candle's body sits inside the previous candle's range.
The price is churning in place rather than progressing. Real
continuation stacks — each candle occupies new space. Overlap means
the market is negotiating, not advancing.

### A single candle that barely tries

One candle after the One Candle pushes slightly in the right direction
— a tiny body, a modest gain — and then the market stalls or reverses.
That single weak candle is not continuation. It is the market
exhausting its last remaining effort before giving up. Continuation
must look genuine, not like a formality.

### The character of the move changed

Before the One Candle, the candles were large, fast, and decisive.
After it, the candles are cautious, tentative, and exploratory. Even
if the direction is technically the same, the mood is different. The
move before the One Candle felt impulsive. The move after feels
negotiated. When the character changes, the continuation is not
really continuing what came before — it is something different wearing
the same direction.
